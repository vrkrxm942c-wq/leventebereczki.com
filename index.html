How I got here

I started doing my own accounting at 14, on paper, to track my progress towards my first car. Then I took a detour to philology, which turned into an unlikely asset in designing AI systems and in legal-adjacent work, and eventually moved on to business studies.

In 2021 I co-founded my first startup, Scraping Robotics (interview, in Hungarian), as CFO and later CEO: lasers and machine learning to automate industrial scraping, a precision metal-finishing job still done by hand. We won a national university startup demo day, raised two VC rounds and built a working version. We spent years perfecting the product and got to present it across Europe and North America, but in the end faced a rapidly shrinking market. We could have used the remaining money to land a few more clients and keep going as a small business with no meaningful upside, leaving our investors with almost nothing. Instead we closed the company and returned a meaningful share of their capital, with me acting as liquidator.

One of my previous investors then offered me a finance job, and my operator career started. I went from financial planning to interim head of finance and back office at a US startup with entities on both sides of the Atlantic, cut accounting costs by 80%, took payroll processing from 4 days to 6 hours, and led the finance side of a restructuring that extended runway by 12 months. Meanwhile I completed my master's in accounting, and for my thesis I designed a controlling system for asset-light, early-stage European companies.

Across these roles I've both led and supported fundraises, cleaned up accounting and cap tables, and coordinated litigation, labor and commercial law matters across the US and multiple European jurisdictions. All this led to the idea behind Ravnar: a system that knows what input triggers what and acts on it, and only asks you when a decision needs you.
